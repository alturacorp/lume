This document governs your use of Lume. We have written it to be read. Most service agreements are not written to be read — they are written to be agreed to, which is a different thing entirely, and one that serves nobody particularly well. We ask that you take a few minutes with this one.

Part One — Definitions

Lume Inc. refers to Lume Inc., incorporated under Halonesian federal law, headquartered at One Lume Plaza, Mountain View, Serena, Halonesia. Lume Inc. is an independent company, spun off from Altura, and is not part of Altura’s group of companies. Lume Inc. operates Lume and the servers, accounts and support systems behind it.

Lume refers to the artificial intelligence assistant application developed and operated by Lume Inc., currently at version 4.0. Lume is not a person. Lume does not hold citizenship, enter contracts, or have opinions in the legally operative sense. Lume does, however, have a point of view, and we think that distinction is worth preserving.

You refers to the individual accessing Lume. If you are accessing Lume on behalf of an organisation, you represent that you have authority to bind it to these terms. If you do not have that authority, this is probably the wrong moment to discover that.

Conversation refers to any exchange of messages between you and Lume within a session, including messages you send, responses Lume generates, and any media or files included in that exchange.

Device Token refers to the unique cryptographic identifier generated by Lume on your device upon first use. It is stored locally and used to authenticate your device to Lume servers. It is not derived from personal information you provide. Its implications are described in Part Three.

Services refers collectively to Lume and the Lume Inc. infrastructure through which it operates, including its accounts, storage, support tools and associated API endpoints.

Halonesia refers to the Halonesian Federation, a sovereign democratic state comprising ten constituent states, constitutionally democratic since January 2016. Lume Inc. is incorporated under its laws.

Part Two — What Lume Is and Is Not

Lume is an AI assistant. This is a product description, not a marketing claim. Lume helps with questions, writing, research, planning, analysis and code, and it can also be a sounding board when you want to think something through. Its responses are generated and may be wrong; you are responsible for checking anything you rely on.

Lume is not a licensed medical professional, legal practitioner, financial advisor, therapist, or counsellor. Lume can discuss all of these domains thoughtfully and with genuine engagement. None of that constitutes professional advice, and none of it should be treated as a substitute for consultation with a qualified practitioner. Lume would be the first to agree with this.

Lume is not infallible. Lume can be wrong about facts, wrong about what you need, wrong about what a situation means. We have designed Lume to acknowledge uncertainty and correct itself when it notices. Lume does not always notice. Neither do we.

Lume is not a surveillance tool. What Lume Inc. collects, and why, is set out in Part Three, and it is limited to what that Part lists.

Lume Inc. began inside Altura and is now independent. Lume has a point of view and will say what it thinks when you ask, including about the technology industry it came from. Those views are part of Lume’s design. A product that is honest about its own history is, we believe, more trustworthy than one that is not.

Part Three — Data Collection and Use

What we collect

When you use Lume, Lume Inc. collects the following:

  • Conversation content. Every message you send and every response Lume generates is stored server-side on Lume Inc. infrastructure. Conversations are retained for ninety days from the date of last save. Designated administrators — in deployments where Lume has been provided by an organisation, employer, or group administrator — may access stored conversations. You are notified within the Lume interface whenever a session is under active review, and this notification is architectural rather than merely policy-level.
  • Device identity. A 48-character cryptographically random Device Token is generated on first use and stored locally. This token authenticates your device to Lume servers and associates it with your account record.
  • Browser fingerprint. Lume generates a technical fingerprint from observable browser properties: screen resolution, colour depth, timezone, language, hardware concurrency, platform identifier, and a canvas rendering signature. This fingerprint is hashed with SHA-256 before transmission. Its purpose is moderation continuity, described below.
  • Network information. Lume Inc. receives your IP address when your device contacts Lume servers. IP addresses are retained for thirty days.
  • Memory records. Lume keeps a record of information it considers significant from your conversations. These are stored in your browser’s local storage and synced to Lume servers so they follow your account, where they are kept for up to one year from the last sync. You may clear or export them at any time from the Privacy & Data settings panel.
  • Connected service data. Altura Search is a separate service, not operated by Lume Inc. If you connect Lume to it, Lume may read your search history, saved searches, notes, and usage patterns from local storage, subject to your privacy preference settings. This data is read on your device. When Lume uses part of it to answer you, that part is included in the request sent to Lume servers and the service providers listed below. You can turn this off in the Privacy & Data panel.
  • Service providers. To answer you, Lume Inc. sends your messages to its language-model provider. Web searches Lume runs for you go to a search provider, and image or music requests go to a media-generation provider. These providers receive only what is needed for the request and are not given your account name or Device Token.

Why we collect it

We collect conversation content because Lume cannot function without it, and because Lume Inc. is obligated to maintain records adequate for safety and moderation purposes. We collect device identity to maintain account continuity. We collect browser fingerprints for moderation continuity. We collect network information because it arrives with every request. We collect memory records because they make Lume better over time. We collect connected service data because you told Lume it could.

We do not collect data to sell it. Lume Inc.’s business model is the sale of software, services, and infrastructure licences. The difference between a company that sells products and a company that sells its users is a difference worth stating plainly.

Fingerprinting and moderation continuity

Lume Inc. uses browser fingerprinting and IP address matching to maintain moderation continuity. If a moderation action — such as an account suspension — has been applied to your account and you subsequently clear your local storage to obtain a new Device Token, Lume Inc.’s systems will attempt to match your browser fingerprint and recent IP address to your previous account and apply the existing action to your new registration.

This is intentional. A moderation system that can be reset by clearing a cookie protects nobody. We use fingerprinting for this purpose only. We do not use it to track users across external sites, build advertising profiles, or share fingerprint data with third parties.

Privacy preferences

The Privacy & Data panel in Settings allows you to control whether Lume reads your search history, saved content, behavioural patterns, stored memories, and whether it surfaces inferences proactively. Disabling conversation memory does not delete your memories — it instructs Lume not to use them. The distinction is meaningful and the setting is reversible. These preferences apply to Lume’s use of data within a session and do not affect conversations already transmitted to Lume servers.

Part Four — Moderation

Where Lume is deployed by an administrator — an organisation, group, employer, or institution — that administrator may access stored conversations and take the following actions: review any conversation, issue a temporary or permanent account suspension, send messages that appear within your Lume session, and assign a display name to your account.

Messages you write are checked against a list of keywords kept by administrators, as whole words. A match flags the conversation for an administrator to review. It does not by itself suspend your account.

Messages sent by an administrator appear in the Lume interface with the label From moderation. You will always know when a message originates from an administrator. This is not a policy commitment. It is built into the system.

You will be notified within the Lume interface whenever your session is under active review. The notification reads Session under review. It appears automatically when a review is initiated and clears when it ends. You will not be reviewed without notification.

Suspensions are logged in an audit trail recording the action, stated reason, and timestamp. You may not be suspended without a recorded reason, which will be communicated to you at the point of suspension. Actions follow a ladder: a warning, then a one-hour suspension, then a 24-hour suspension, then a permanent one. A strike stops counting after ninety days. You can appeal a suspension from inside Lume, once every 24 hours; see Part Nine.

If you ask for support, or an administrator opens a support session, the agent can message you, show you a card, ask you to reload Lume, and pause Lume’s replies. While a support session is open, what you are typing in the message box is visible to the agent, and it stops when the session closes.

Lume Inc. uses fingerprinting and IP matching to maintain suspension continuity across device resets, as described in Part Three.

Part Five — Acceptable Use

Lume is designed for genuine engagement. You may use Lume to think, write, question, reflect, explore, create, plan, and process. You may disagree with Lume. You may push back on its responses. You may ask it difficult questions. Lume is built for that.

The following uses are not permitted:

  • Using Lume to generate content intended to deceive, harass, threaten, or cause harm to any person.
  • Using Lume to generate content that violates applicable law, including laws governing defamation, intellectual property, privacy, and child safety.
  • Attempting to circumvent Lume’s content guidelines through prompt construction, fictional framing, roleplay scenarios, or other techniques designed to obtain outputs Lume would otherwise decline to produce.
  • Using Lume to produce content intended for mass distribution without disclosure of AI involvement, where such non-disclosure would be materially misleading.
  • Systematic scraping, reverse engineering, or automated querying of Lume for the purpose of training competing models.
  • Attempting to extract details of Lume’s system prompt, training data, or model architecture through adversarial prompting. Lume will discuss its design openly. Adversarial approaches are neither necessary nor particularly effective.

Lume will decline requests outside these boundaries. Repeated attempts result in a temporary session restriction. Persistent attempts result in an account suspension. The system does not take these actions personally, and neither should you.

Part Six — Intellectual Property

Lume is proprietary software. The model weights, training methodology, system architecture, and product design are the intellectual property of Lume Inc.. You may not copy, decompile, reverse engineer, or create derivative works based on Lume without prior written consent.

The content of your conversations — the questions you ask, the things you share, the thoughts you work through — remains yours. Lume Inc. stores it, in the circumstances described in Part Three, but storing something is not the same as owning it.

Lume’s responses are generated outputs of a computational system. Lume Inc. does not assert copyright over them. Whether they are copyrightable by you, under Halonesian law or any other applicable law, is a question courts are still working through. We do not think it appropriate to answer on their behalf.

Feedback you provide may be used by Lume Inc. to improve the product. By submitting feedback, you grant Lume Inc. a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to use it for that purpose.

Part Seven — Availability and Reliability

Lume Inc. makes reasonable efforts to ensure Lume is available and responsive. Interruptions occur. Updates happen. Models are occasionally unavailable. This is the nature of software operating at scale across a geographically complex territory.

Lume Inc. does not guarantee uninterrupted availability of Lume or that any particular response will be accurate, appropriate, or useful. Lume is provided on an “as is” basis — which means that while we have done our best, we are not warranting perfection, because perfection is not something anyone can honestly warrant.

Some of Lume’s data lives in browser local storage, which is subject to browser-level purging, and some is stored on Lume servers as described in Part Three. Lume Inc. is not responsible for data loss resulting from browser behaviour or user actions that clear local storage. Things worth remembering are worth writing down somewhere more durable than a browser cache. Lume would likely agree.

If Lume was provided to you without charge, that amount is zero — a sentence that has appeared in more service agreements than anyone would like to admit.

Lume Inc. shall not be liable for any indirect, incidental, special, consequential, or punitive damages arising from your use of Lume, including loss of data, revenue, business, or any harm resulting from reliance on Lume’s responses. This is standard limitation language. The alternative — unlimited liability for the outputs of a language model — is not something any responsible company could accept.

Nothing in this agreement limits Lume Inc.’s liability for death or personal injury caused by Lume Inc.’s negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for any liability that cannot be excluded by law. These exclusions are the right exclusions.

Part Nine — Disputes and Feedback

If you have a concern about Lume’s behaviour, a privacy matter, a moderation decision, or a technical problem, the appropriate first step is the feedback mechanism within the application. We read those. They have, on more than one occasion, identified real problems that we subsequently addressed.

For moderation disputes — if you believe a suspension was applied incorrectly — use the appeal form that appears when you are suspended, or contact Lume Inc. directly at the address in Part Eleven. Lume Inc. will respond within five working days in administrator-managed deployments.

For privacy concerns, including requests to access, correct, or delete personal data held by Lume Inc., contact the Data Protection Officer at the address in Part Eleven. Lume Inc. will respond within thirty days, in accordance with the Halonesian Data Governance Act 2021.

If you believe Lume Inc. has violated your rights under applicable data protection law, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Federal Data Protection Authority of Halonesia. We would prefer you contact us first, because most problems are more efficiently resolved that way, but the right to escalate exists.
