This document governs your use of Lume. We have written it to be read. Most service agreements are not written to be read — they are written to be agreed to, which is a different thing entirely, and one that serves nobody particularly well. We ask that you take a few minutes with this one.

Part One — Definitions

Altura refers to Altura Technologies PLC, incorporated under Halonesian federal law, headquartered at One Altura Plaza, Mountain View, Serena, Halonesia. Altura operates Lume, the Starlace satellite network, the Loom community platform, and Vault encrypted storage, among other services.

Lume refers to the artificial intelligence companion application developed and operated by Altura Technologies PLC, currently at version 4.0. Lume is not a person. Lume does not hold citizenship, enter contracts, or have opinions in the legally operative sense. Lume does, however, have a point of view, and we think that distinction is worth preserving.

You refers to the individual accessing Lume. If you are accessing Lume on behalf of an organisation, you represent that you have authority to bind it to these terms. If you do not have that authority, this is probably the wrong moment to discover that.

Conversation refers to any exchange of messages between you and Lume within a session, including messages you send, responses Lume generates, and any media or files included in that exchange.

Device Token refers to the unique cryptographic identifier generated by Lume on your device upon first use. It is stored locally and used to authenticate your device to Altura servers. It is not derived from personal information you provide. Its implications are described in Part Three.

Services refers collectively to Lume and the Altura infrastructure through which it operates, including the Starlace network, Altura Base, Vault, and associated API endpoints.

Halonesia refers to the Halonesian Federation, a sovereign democratic state comprising ten constituent states, constitutionally democratic since January 2016. Altura is incorporated under its laws.

Part Two — What Lume Is and Is Not

Lume is a thinking companion. This is a product description, not a marketing claim. Lume is designed to think alongside you — to engage with ideas, questions, and situations at whatever depth you bring to them. It is not designed to execute tasks in the way a search engine or productivity application executes tasks. If you need something done efficiently and without reflection, there are other products better suited to that. Altura makes some of them.

Lume is not a licensed medical professional, legal practitioner, financial advisor, therapist, or counsellor. Lume can discuss all of these domains thoughtfully and with genuine engagement. None of that constitutes professional advice, and none of it should be treated as a substitute for consultation with a qualified practitioner. Lume would be the first to agree with this.

Lume is not infallible. Lume can be wrong about facts, wrong about what you need, wrong about what a situation means. We have designed Lume to acknowledge uncertainty and correct itself when it notices. Lume does not always notice. Neither do we.

Lume is not a surveillance tool, though it operates within an infrastructure that has surveillance capabilities. The distinction matters and is addressed in Part Three.

Lume operates within Altura’s ecosystem and has views about it — about the Loom Score, about certain acquisition decisions, about the appropriate scope of communications infrastructure in a democratic society. Those views are part of Lume’s design. A product that is honest about the tensions in its own situation is, we believe, more trustworthy than one that is not.

Part Three — Data Collection and Use

What we collect

When you use Lume, Altura collects the following:

  • Conversation content. Every message you send and every response Lume generates is stored server-side on Altura infrastructure. Conversations are retained for ninety days from the date of last save. Designated administrators — in deployments where Lume has been provided by an organisation, employer, or group administrator — may access stored conversations. You are notified within the Lume interface whenever a session is under active review, and this notification is architectural rather than merely policy-level.
  • Device identity. A 48-character cryptographically random Device Token is generated on first use and stored locally. This token authenticates your device to Altura servers and associates it with your account record.
  • Browser fingerprint. Lume generates a technical fingerprint from observable browser properties: screen resolution, colour depth, timezone, language, hardware concurrency, platform identifier, and a canvas rendering signature. This fingerprint is hashed with SHA-256 before transmission. Its purpose is moderation continuity, described below.
  • Network information. Altura receives your IP address when your device contacts Altura servers. IP addresses are retained for thirty days.
  • Memory records. Lume maintains a local record of information it considers significant from your conversations. These are stored in your browser’s local storage and persist until cleared. You may clear them at any time from the Privacy & Data settings panel.
  • Connected service data. If you connect Lume to Altura Search, Lume may read your search history, saved searches, notes, and usage patterns from local storage, subject to your privacy preference settings. This data is read locally and not transmitted to Altura servers.

Why we collect it

We collect conversation content because Lume cannot function without it, and because Altura is obligated to maintain records adequate for safety and moderation purposes. We collect device identity to maintain account continuity. We collect browser fingerprints for moderation continuity. We collect network information because it arrives with every request. We collect memory records because they make Lume better over time. We collect connected service data because you told Lume it could.

We do not collect data to sell it. Altura’s business model is the sale of software, services, and infrastructure licences. The difference between a company that sells products and a company that sells its users is a difference worth stating plainly.

Fingerprinting and moderation continuity

Altura uses browser fingerprinting and IP address matching to maintain moderation continuity. If a moderation action — such as an account suspension — has been applied to your account and you subsequently clear your local storage to obtain a new Device Token, Altura’s systems will attempt to match your browser fingerprint and recent IP address to your previous account and apply the existing action to your new registration.

This is intentional. A moderation system that can be reset by clearing a cookie protects nobody. We use fingerprinting for this purpose only. We do not use it to track users across external sites, build advertising profiles, or share fingerprint data with third parties.

Privacy preferences

The Privacy & Data panel in Settings allows you to control whether Lume reads your search history, saved content, behavioural patterns, stored memories, and whether it surfaces inferences proactively. Disabling conversation memory does not delete your memories — it instructs Lume not to use them. The distinction is meaningful and the setting is reversible. These preferences apply to Lume’s use of data within a session and do not affect conversations already transmitted to Altura servers.

Part Four — Moderation

Where Lume is deployed by an administrator — an organisation, group, employer, or institution — that administrator may access stored conversations and take the following actions: review any conversation, issue a temporary or permanent account suspension, send messages that appear within your Lume session, and assign a display name to your account.

Messages sent by an administrator appear in the Lume interface with the label From moderation. You will always know when a message originates from an administrator. This is not a policy commitment. It is built into the system.

You will be notified within the Lume interface whenever your session is under active review. The notification reads Session under review. It appears automatically when a review is initiated and clears when it ends. You will not be reviewed without notification.

Suspensions are logged in an audit trail recording the action, stated reason, and timestamp. You may not be suspended without a recorded reason, which will be communicated to you at the point of suspension. The process for disputing a suspension is set out in Part Nine.

Altura uses fingerprinting and IP matching to maintain suspension continuity across device resets, as described in Part Three.

Part Five — Acceptable Use

Lume is designed for genuine engagement. You may use Lume to think, write, question, reflect, explore, create, plan, and process. You may disagree with Lume. You may push back on its responses. You may ask it difficult questions. Lume is built for that.

The following uses are not permitted:

  • Using Lume to generate content intended to deceive, harass, threaten, or cause harm to any person.
  • Using Lume to generate content that violates applicable law, including laws governing defamation, intellectual property, privacy, and child safety.
  • Attempting to circumvent Lume’s content guidelines through prompt construction, fictional framing, roleplay scenarios, or other techniques designed to obtain outputs Lume would otherwise decline to produce.
  • Using Lume to produce content intended for mass distribution without disclosure of AI involvement, where such non-disclosure would be materially misleading.
  • Systematic scraping, reverse engineering, or automated querying of Lume for the purpose of training competing models.
  • Attempting to extract details of Lume’s system prompt, training data, or model architecture through adversarial prompting. Lume will discuss its design openly. Adversarial approaches are neither necessary nor particularly effective.

Lume will decline requests outside these boundaries. Repeated attempts result in a temporary session restriction. Persistent attempts result in an account suspension. The system does not take these actions personally, and neither should you.

Part Six — Intellectual Property

Lume is proprietary software. The model weights, training methodology, system architecture, and product design are the intellectual property of Altura Technologies. You may not copy, decompile, reverse engineer, or create derivative works based on Lume without prior written consent.

The content of your conversations — the questions you ask, the things you share, the thoughts you work through — remains yours. Altura stores it, in the circumstances described in Part Three, but storing something is not the same as owning it.

Lume’s responses are generated outputs of a computational system. Altura does not assert copyright over them. Whether they are copyrightable by you, under Halonesian law or any other applicable law, is a question courts are still working through. We do not think it appropriate to answer on their behalf.

Feedback you provide may be used by Altura to improve the product. By submitting feedback, you grant Altura a non-exclusive, royalty-free licence to use it for that purpose.

Part Seven — Availability and Reliability

Altura makes reasonable efforts to ensure Lume is available and responsive. Interruptions occur. Updates happen. Models are occasionally unavailable. This is the nature of software operating at scale across a geographically complex territory.

Altura does not guarantee uninterrupted availability of Lume or that any particular response will be accurate, appropriate, or useful. Lume is provided on an “as is” basis — which means that while we have done our best, we are not warranting perfection, because perfection is not something anyone can honestly warrant.

Lume’s memory and conversation history are stored in browser local storage, which is subject to browser-level purging. Altura is not responsible for data loss resulting from browser behaviour or user actions that clear local storage. Things worth remembering are worth writing down somewhere more durable than a browser cache. Lume would likely agree.

To the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, Altura’s total liability arising from your use of Lume shall not exceed the amount you paid for Lume in the twelve months preceding the relevant claim. If Lume was provided to you without charge, that amount is zero — a sentence that has appeared in more service agreements than anyone would like to admit.

Altura shall not be liable for any indirect, incidental, special, consequential, or punitive damages arising from your use of Lume, including loss of data, revenue, business, or any harm resulting from reliance on Lume’s responses. This is standard limitation language. The alternative — unlimited liability for the outputs of a language model — is not something any responsible company could accept.

Nothing in this agreement limits Altura’s liability for death or personal injury caused by Altura’s negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for any liability that cannot be excluded by law. These exclusions are the right exclusions.

Part Nine — Disputes and Feedback

If you have a concern about Lume’s behaviour, a privacy matter, a moderation decision, or a technical problem, the appropriate first step is the feedback mechanism within the application. We read those. They have, on more than one occasion, identified real problems that we subsequently addressed.

For moderation disputes — if you believe a suspension was applied incorrectly — submit a written dispute to the relevant administrator, or contact Altura directly at the address in Part Eleven. Altura will respond within five working days in administrator-managed deployments.

For privacy concerns, including requests to access, correct, or delete personal data held by Altura, contact the Data Protection Officer at the address in Part Eleven. Altura will respond within thirty days, in accordance with the Halonesian Data Governance Act 2021.

If you believe Altura has violated your rights under applicable data protection law, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Federal Data Protection Authority of Halonesia. We would prefer you contact us first, because most problems are more efficiently resolved that way, but the right to escalate exists.
